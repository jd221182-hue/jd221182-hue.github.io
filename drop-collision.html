Should you drop collision coverage?

On an older car, the most an insurer will pay after a total loss is roughly the car's value minus your deductible. This calculator compares that maximum payout to what you pay each year for collision and comprehensive.

The formula

maximum payout = car value − deductible
cost ratio = yearly premium ÷ maximum payout

With the example numbers, the maximum payout is $4,000 and you pay 15% of that every year. A common personal rule of thumb is to look hard at dropping the coverage once the ratio passes about 10%, but there's no official cutoff.

Before you drop it

  • If there's a loan or lease, the lender almost always requires the coverage.
  • Ask whether you could replace the car tomorrow without hardship.
  • Keep liability and uninsured motorist coverage; those protect you, not the car.
  • Raising the deductible is a middle step that often lowers the cost.
